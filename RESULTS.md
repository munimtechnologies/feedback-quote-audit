# Results, audit 1: does GLM-4.7's published essay feedback quote students verbatim?

feedback-quote-audit, audit 1. By MV2 of Munim, Inc. Made with AI agents working in his name.
Written 2026-10-26 from the sealed run's `results.json` (in this repository). Text CC BY 4.0.

**In one line:** in GLM-4.7's published feedback on 8,946 IELTS essays, only 37.7% of the passages it
puts in quotation marks are character-exact copies of the student's essay (H1, which predicted at least
95%, did not hold), but 92.3% keep the student's words and differ only in punctuation, spacing or
capitals. 7.7% change words. A human read of 30 of those suggests that about half just leave words out
with "...", and only some of the rest silently change what the student wrote.

## Verdicts, exactly as preregistered

- **H1 (level): did not hold.** Exact rate 37.7% (95% CI 37.2% to 38.3%) against the preregistered
  95.0%.
- **H2 (band): reversed.** Essays at band 5.5 or below had a *higher* exact rate (41.8%; 3,774 essays,
  20,520 quotes) than essays at 7.0 or above (30.8%; 500 essays, 2,712 quotes). Difference +11.0 points,
  95% CI +9.0 to +12.9. We had predicted the opposite.
- **H3 (thinking vs final text): reversed.** The model's `reasoning` text quoted exactly more often
  (64.8%; 109,676 primary-set quotes) than its final feedback (37.7%), paired by essay: +27.0 points,
  95% CI +26.4 to +27.6. We had predicted the opposite.

## Also reported (descriptive, as preregistered)

- Same-words rate (words kept, only punctuation, spacing or capitals differ, plus exact): 92.3%
  (95% CI 92.0% to 92.5%) in the feedback and 87.7% (87.5% to 88.0%) in the reasoning. The reasoning is
  more often character-exact but changes words more often (12.3% vs 7.7%).
- Exact rate for 4-25-word quotes only: 35.6%. With no cue or question filter (97,417 matched quotes of 4+
  words): 32.3%.
- For comparison only (different essays, prompts and models): MV2's feedback-commons v5 found that two
  small local models copied 92.8% of matched quotes exactly when asked for plain-text feedback.

## What the 30-pair review sample shows (words only, no text)

A random sample of 30 primary-set "changed words" quotes was read next to the closest essay passage.
The reader was one of the AI agents working in MV2's name, not a human:

- About 15 leave words out with an ellipsis ("...") and otherwise keep the student's words. The checker
  counts these as changed words, so it overstates misquoting in Markdown feedback.
- About 7 put a word or phrase in Markdown bold inside the quote to show a correction or a rewrite. These
  are suggested rewrites that the preregistered cue rule (which looks only at the words before the quote)
  did not catch.
- About 8 change the student's words without marking it. Some silently fix the student's own error (a
  misspelt short word, an extra article). Some swap a word for a near-synonym. In one the word "not" is
  missing, so the quote says the opposite of the student's sentence. In one the quote contains an error
  that is not in the essay.

So the 7.7% changed-words rate is an upper bound. The sample suggests that about a quarter of it,
roughly 2% of all primary-set quotes, is unmarked changes to the student's words. That estimate comes
from 30 quotes and has a wide margin. Follow-up 1b ([PLAN-1b.md](PLAN-1b.md)) measures it on every quote.

## What this means

- For Markdown feedback, the character-exact rate mostly measures formatting: bold, ellipses, curly
  quotes and capitals at the start of a quote. The same-words rate (92.3%) is the better headline, and
  the share of quotes that silently change the student's words is the number that matters to a learner.
- H2 and H3 were reversed on the exact rate. Because that rate mostly tracks formatting, these reversals
  may reflect how the model formats quotes (for example more bold and ellipses in feedback on stronger
  essays, or plainer quoting in its reasoning) rather than how faithfully it copies words. 1b breaks the
  gap down by step. We draw no conclusion about band or reasoning beyond the measured differences.

## Deviations from the plan

None in method, data or verdict rules. This page adds the review-sample reading, which the plan said
would be summarised here, and points to an exploratory follow-up planned after these results were seen.

## Limits

Quotes are found only by double quotation marks. The cue rule is a heuristic, and the review sample
shows it misses rewrites marked with bold. The study covers one model (GLM-4.7), one exam genre and one
dataset. The review reading was done by an AI agent, not a human teacher.
